The Brief — What To Write Monday

Modeled on top vs bottom posts by buyer count
Your format is right. Your topic altitude and who you address are what flip the room.

Best and worst posts use the same arrow-list, lead-magnet structure. When you talk to beginners ("no coding required, you can't break it, ship today, free tools") buyers show up 44–52 per post. When you flex about running your business ("I replaced my team", "agent memory architecture", "anti-sales for experts") buyers collapse to 4–6 and peers + competitors fill the comments. → Write for the person who hasn't started yet.

↑ 3 Angles To Lean Into

01"You can't break it" — kill the fear of starting
Name the exact thing beginners are scared of ("what if I break something", "I'm not technical") and dismantle it. Highest-trust buyer angle you have.
02Free, one-command tools (comment-to-DM lists)
"Here are X open-source Claude skills most people don't know exist. All free. Comment WORD." Zero-risk, deployable — buyers self-select by commenting.
03"Here's the exact system" → visible result + income
Tie one tactical Claude Code workflow to a result a beginner wants (followers, first $, a finished thing), with named tools and same-day shippability.

↓ 3 To Cut Or Adjust

01"I replaced my team / $5 a day" flexes
Reframe: pick the ONE piece a beginner could build this week and teach that, in their shoes — "here's the first agent to build, start here".
02Advanced agent-architecture deep-dives
Cut or translate. Virtual filesystems, agent memory, context engineering pull engineers + competitors. If it isn't "ship today, no code", it isn't for your buyer.
03Sales playbooks for "experts" & "business owners"
Re-aim. Target the beginner wanting their first inbound lead. Drop "business owners" / "experts" from hooks — say "you" and "if you're just starting".
