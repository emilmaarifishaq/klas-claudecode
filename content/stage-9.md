## 🧱 Pick Your Content Pillars (Never Stare At A Blank Screen)

![Pick Your Content Pillars](/images/stage9-pillars.png)

> **You'll walk away with:** 3–5 pillars that turn 'what do I post?' into 'which pillar is it today?' — the single biggest burnout killer.

A **pillar** is a recurring theme you post on. Pick 3–5 and you're done deciding forever. Creators who work from a clear pillar list find it works because they start from a known theme instead of a blank page — and burn out far less.

- **The play** — something you made (Stage 3–9 work)
- **The lesson** — one thing you learned doing it
- **The mistake** — what broke, how you fixed it
- **The contrarian take** — "you don't need to learn to code."
- **The offer** — soft "want one of these? comment [WORD]."

Don't overthink it. Pick yours, write them down, and let Claude pressure-test them. Every future post is now just "pillar #2, this week's build" — a slot to fill, not a blank page.

**Prompts:**

> I build software by describing it to Claude (no coding background). Audience: [who]. Propose 5 content pillars I can rotate forever — for each, give the pillar name, why it works, and 3 example post ideas from the kind of builds I do. Plain-spoken, no influencer hype.

> Here are my draft pillars: [paste]. Which is weakest and why? Replace it with a stronger one that still fits a builder who ships in public, and show me 3 sample posts for the replacement.

## 📅 Batch A Whole Week In One Sitting

![Batch A Whole Week In One Sitting](/images/stage9-batch.png)

> **You'll walk away with:** a full week of posts produced in one focused session — so daily-you only has to hit 'post'.

Batching is the whole game. You sit down **once**, stay in one headspace, and produce the week — instead of fighting the blank page every day.

- **Block 1 — Pick (10 min):** choose one real build from this week + which pillars it feeds
- **Block 2 — Generate (25 min):** feed the build to Claude, get the week's posts in one shot
- **Block 3 — Cut (5 min):** read in plain words — "cut the jargon", "make day 5 a question." You curate, not rewrite.
- **Block 4 — Park (10 min):** drop each post into your calendar/sheet. Done for the week.

**What beginners get wrong:**
- They "batch" by posting one post really well, then nothing. Batch the **set**, not one piece.
- They wait for a great build. The boring build batches the entire week — pick the one you have, post it anyway.
- They redo it daily. The point of batching is that daily-you makes **zero** decisions.

**Prompts:**

> Here's a build I did this week and roughly what happened: [paste 3–4 sentences]. Using these pillars [paste pillars], generate a full week of social posts in ONE response: 1 short-form video script (4-part: hook / content / 2–3 value points / soft CTA), 2 X posts, 1 LinkedIn post, 1 carousel outline, 1 soft-offer post. Each standalone, hook first, in my plain-spoken voice. Label each by day and pillar.

> From those, give me 10 scroll-stopping hooks, pick the strongest 3 and say why each works for a mute viewer who's never heard of Claude Code.

## 🗓️ Lock A Cadence You'll Actually Keep

![Lock A Cadence You'll Actually Keep](/images/stage9-cadence.png)

> **You'll walk away with:** a written posting cadence committed to — so posting stops being a decision.

A cadence is your posting schedule written down and committed to, *not* as a goal but as a minimum. The minimum is what you do even when you don't feel like it.

**One anchor per week** = the build video. That's the keystone. **2–4 repurposed posts** spread across the week from that same build.

Rough per-platform sanity check for 2026: short-form video ~3–5x/week works on Reels and YouTube Shorts; TikTok rewards ~daily. Pick **one** platform to start — one done beats four half-done.

- **5x/week:** the growth lane — one post per weekday. Only if you can batch 5 in one sitting reliably.
- **3x/week:** the sustainable lane — Monday/Wednesday/Friday. The sweet spot for most people.
- **1x/week:** the floor — one post, any day, without fail. Start here if you've never been consistent before.

**The rule:** pick the cadence one level *below* what you think you can do. Consistency at 3x beats bursts at 5x followed by silence.

**Prompt:**

> Design a sustainable 1-week posting cadence for a solo builder who ships in public, posting only on [platform]. One anchor build-video + 2–4 repurposed posts. Give me a day-by-day table I can paste into a Google Sheet, plus a 60-minute weekly batch checklist. Keep it survivable for someone with a day job.

## 📜 What A Hook Actually Is — The Contract You Make In A Second

![What A Hook Actually Is](/images/stage9-hook-contract.png)

> **You'll walk away with:** a working definition of a hook that survives muted feeds, doom scrolling, and a viewer who has never heard of you — so every post you write from this point forward starts with the part that decides whether anything else gets read.

A hook is a **contract**. In the first three seconds, or the first eight words, you are telling the viewer: "If you stay, I will deliver this specific thing." The hook makes a promise. The body of the post pays the promise off.

So the question stops being "how do I write a catchy hook?" and becomes **"what contract am I making in this post?"** Ask this constantly. "What is the specific thing this post is promising in the first line?" — that question tells you what to cut and what to keep.

### Why the first 3 seconds rule the whole post

On muted feeds, the first frame must do two things: stop the thumb (visual pattern interrupt — a face, a screen, a number, motion) and open a loop the brain wants to close (a question, a contradiction, a stake, a reveal). Miss either and the viewer is gone before your audio even loaded.

Text posts are the same game in slow motion. The first line is what's visible above the "see more" fold. If the brain can't pinpoint where the ride is going — somewhere specific, somewhere worth knowing — it scrolls.

### What a hook is NOT

- **Not a summary.** "Today I want to talk about content hooks." That's a table of contents, not a hook. You just told the brain what's coming. The brain says thanks and leaves.
- **Not a greeting.** "Hey guys, what's up." You're a stranger asking for 30 seconds of attention. Earn it before the small talk.
- **Not your title.** A title describes. A hook promises. "5 Tips For Better Posts" describes a list. "I tried 5 hook formulas — only 1 actually worked" promises a story with a payoff.

### Why builder hooks are easier than influencer hooks

You have the rarest thing in 2026 social — **proof**. You actually built the thing. You can show the screen. You can say "20 minutes, no code, here it is" and the viewer can see whether you're lying within frame two. Influencers have to manufacture trust; builders just press play.

**Prompts:**

> Here are 3 hooks from my recent posts: [paste]. For each, tell me (a) the exact promise it's making to the viewer in one line, (b) whether the body of the post pays that promise off, and (c) one stronger version of the hook that makes a sharper, more specific promise. No influencer language — plain-spoken builder voice.

> Pull up 5 random posts in [my niche] from creators bigger than me. Reverse-engineer the *contract* each hook is making — what specifically did it promise the viewer in the first line? Score each 1–10 on how clearly the promise was made and explain.

## 🗂️ The 8 Hook Archetypes — A Taxonomy You Can Rotate

![The 8 Hook Archetypes](/images/stage9-hook-archetypes.png)

> **You'll walk away with:** a clean list of 8 hook archetypes you can rotate through for the rest of your career — with a one-line definition and one builder example for each, so you stop inventing hooks from scratch and start running formulas.

Hooks are not infinite. There are maybe 15 patterns that account for ~85% of every post that has ever gone viral. Here are the eight that work especially well for builders:

1. **Curiosity Gap** — partial promise that forces the viewer to stay to close the loop. *"I found a way to cut my client onboarding from 3 hours to 11 minutes — and it's not an automation."*
2. **Contrarian Claim** — disagree with the common wisdom the viewer holds. *"You don't need to learn to code in 2026. Here's what you actually need."*
3. **Stakes** — cost of not watching, in real terms. *"You're paying $4,800/yr to do a job Claude does free."*
4. **Before/After** — the transformation, fast. *"My proposal used to take 4 hours. Now it takes 9 minutes. Same result."*
5. **Little Promise** — a small, specific, immediately usable thing. *"One prompt that writes better LinkedIn hooks than I do."*
6. **Revealed Mistake** — confession that makes the viewer feel smart for staying. *"I spent 3 months learning to code for nothing. Here's what I should have done instead."*
7. **Enemy** — a shared frustration or obstacle the viewer recognises. *"Tech gatekeeping has a real cost — and most people don't see it until they've already paid it."*
8. **Status Shift** — the viewer becomes someone different by the end. *"I went from 'I'm not technical' to shipping an app in 4 days. This is what changed."*

**The deeper move:** every archetype maps to a different emotion — Curiosity-gap = unresolved tension. Contrarian = surprise + permission. Stakes = fear of loss. Before/after = relief. Pick the emotion you want the viewer to feel, then pick the archetype that delivers it.

**Prompts:**

> Here's a real build I shipped this week: [paste 2–3 sentences]. Write me 8 hooks, ONE for each of these archetypes, labeled: 1) Curiosity Gap, 2) Contrarian Claim, 3) Stakes, 4) Before/After, 5) Little Promise, 6) Revealed Mistake, 7) Enemy, 8) Status Shift. Each hook must work for my specific build. Plain-spoken builder voice.

> Of those 8, which 2 archetypes fit THIS specific build best and why? And which 2 would feel forced or fake for this build and why? Be brutal — I'd rather drop an archetype than fake one.

## 🔬 The Hook Lab — A 20-Minute Weekly Exercise

![The Hook Lab](/images/stage9-hook-lab.png)

> **You'll walk away with:** a structured 20-minute weekly drill — write 10, kill 7, A/B the 3 — so your hook-writing muscle compounds week over week without you needing to feel inspired.

The structure is intentionally narrow: write 10, kill 7, A/B the remaining 3. The discipline of killing 70% of your own hooks is what trains the judgement — because the writing isn't the bottleneck, the editing is.

### The 20-minute structure

- **Minutes 1–5:** Pick this week's build. Write 10 hooks fast.
- **Minutes 5–10 — Kill 7.** Kill anything that could describe ANY post in your niche. Kill any greeting. Kill anything that starts with "I". Kill anything using the word "journey". Kill any hook over 12 words on the first pass. Kill anything that's a tease with no stake.
- **Minutes 10–15 — A/B test.** Pick 2 of the strongest 3 hooks. Post the same body content with hook A on one format (LinkedIn) and hook B on another (newsletter subject line) within 48 hours. Watch which performs.

### Why small-audience A/B testing still works

You need 7 views minimum, not 100,000 followers. The meaningful difference between hook A getting 15 views and hook B getting 48 is real signal. You're not measuring "is this hook viral?" but "is this hook meaningfully better than my other hook." That comparison works at any scale.

Six weeks of Hook Lab = 60 hooks written, 42 killed, 18 sharpened, 12 tested. That's more deliberate practice than most creators get in a year.

**Prompts:**

> Run the Hook Lab with me. Build: [paste]. Write 10 hooks (8 archetype + 2 wild cards). Then immediately critique each one in one line — kill 7 yourself with reasons, keep the strongest 3 and tell me why they survived. Be a brutal editor, not a cheerleader.

> For the 3 survivors: rewrite each one twice. Version A — shorter (max 8 words). Version B — more specific (real numbers, real nouns, real time-bounds). Then recommend which of A or B per hook to actually post and why.

## 🔁 Leverage — One Winning Hook Into A Full Week

![One Winning Hook Into A Full Week](/images/stage9-hook-leverage.png)

> **You'll walk away with:** a repeatable system for turning ONE proven hook into a week of posts across formats — tweet, LinkedIn, short-form video, newsletter subject, podcast title — so a winning hook earns you 7 posts of compounding distribution, not one.

A hook that worked is not a post — it's an **asset**. Once you have a hook that lands, you have proof of concept for the underlying promise. Every format can lean on the same underlying promise, each adapted to the format's rules.

### The 7-format hook-leverage map

Example hook: *"I built a $0 invoice-chaser that collected $4,300 in 8 days while I worked my day job."*

- **Tweet/X:** "$0 invoice-chaser. 8 days. Built it with Claude before work. Here's the 5-step prompt."
- **LinkedIn post:** Open with the near-miss (almost hired a VA), reveal the 4-sentence Claude prompt, soft CTA "comment INVOICE for the full prompt."
- **Short-form video:** "I collected $4,300 in late invoices in 8 days (no code)" → screenshot + terminal. Long-form proof.
- **Podcast/episode title:** "The $0 Invoice Chaser: How A Non-Coder Recovered $4,300 in 8 Days."
- **Thread opener:** "I had $4,300 in outstanding invoices and almost hired a VA. The numbers are real." → Curiosity gap across 6–8 tweets.
- **Newsletter subject:** "I described what I wanted in 4 sentences and got $4,300 back."
- **Community/Skool post:** "Here's the receipts on the $0 invoice chaser. AMA in the comments."

Same core promise, vary the body. Keep the promise identical — adapt the language, length, visual, and CTA to each format's native shape.

Run this for 12 weeks: 12 proven hooks × 7 formats = 84 anchored posts, every one tied to a contract you already know works.

## 🖼️ Hook + Visual — The Right First Frame

![Hook + Visual](/images/stage9-hook-visual.png)

> **You'll walk away with:** a working pairing system between each of the 8 hook archetypes and a matching first frame visual — so the hook lands silently on a muted scroll.

A hook is the contract; the visual is what makes the contract legible in three seconds without the sound off.

### The four visual modes

- **Face.** A human face, looking at camera, with a clear expression — surprise, disbelief, focus. Faces are the strongest pattern interrupt in any feed.
- **Screen.** Your screen recording with something *visibly happening* in frame one — a Claude prompt, code generating, a dashboard lighting up. Low friction, no face needed.
- **Text-on-screen.** A big, bold on-screen line — the hook itself, or a number, or a conclusion. The hook *is* the visual.
- **Motion / Reveal.** Fast cuts, a thing transforming, a before/after wipe. Stops thumbs even when content is weak — but costs trust if the body doesn't pay off.

### The pairing chart — archetype → visual

- **Curiosity Gap** — text-on-screen + face. Text states the partial promise; face holds the unresolved expression.
- **Contrarian** — face + text-on-screen. Face says "I'm about to disagree with the room"; text states the disagreement.
- **Stakes** — text-on-screen + motion. Big bold number with a ticking counter or fast cut.
- **Before/After** — screen + text-on-screen. The transformation shown on screen, the delta as text.
- **Revealed Mistake** — face + text-on-screen. The "I'm about to admit something dumb" expression does the work.
- **Enemy** — face + text-on-screen. Face does the eyebrow-raise; text names the shared enemy.
- **Status Shift** — face + screen. Face shows the after-status; screen shows the proof of how they got there.

**Two rules that survive every platform:**
1. **First frame is the silent contract.** Test with audio off — if the hook and visual don't communicate the promise, the visual is wrong.
2. **Match the visual energy to the archetype's emotion.** Curiosity-gap wants suspense. Stakes wants alarm. Status shift wants confidence.

**Prompts:**

> Here's a hook I want to post + the archetype it is: [paste]. Recommend the strongest visual pairing for the first 3 seconds — face / text-on-screen / screen / motion, or which combo? Tell me literally what should be in frame 1, what text overlay if any, and what the energy of the frame should feel like.

> Critique this first frame visual against the hook it's paired with: [describe visual + hook]. Does the visual communicate the contract silently in 3 seconds with audio off? If not, what specifically would you change in frame 1?

## 🤖 Hook + Claude — Generate 30 Hooks Fast, Then Cut Hard

![Hook + Claude](/images/stage9-hook-claude.png)

> **You'll walk away with:** a Claude workflow for generating 30 hooks in under 5 minutes and, more importantly, the judgement system for picking the 3 that are actually post-able — so the AI is the typist and you are the editor.

### The prompt structure that works

Request hooks across three dimensions — archetype, angle, and length — so the 30 hooks span the actual space of what's possible, not 30 versions of the same idea:

- **Input the build** (2–3 sentences + proof numbers, time, what it replaced)
- **Input your audience** (one line — who is this *for*)
- **Input your voice** (one line — what you sound like, what you don't)
- **Force all 8 archetypes** (~4 per archetype)
- **Force length variety** (10 short under 8 words, 10 medium under 16 words, 10 longer)

### The brutal cut — 30 → 10 → 3

**Pass 1 — 30 to 10, fast.** Strike out any hook that starts with a greeting, summarises the post, uses adjective-stacked influencer language, makes a vague promise, or could have been written about literally any build. You should be at ~10 in 90 seconds.

**Pass 2 — 10 to 3.** Rate the survivors. The hooks you're looking for are specific, promise-first, and use concrete nouns.

### The mistake everyone makes

Posting the first hook Claude returns. Don't. Claude's first attempt regresses toward the average of every hook in its training data — generic. The good hooks are usually #11 through #23 in the batch. Read all 30.

The editor's posture: you are not asking Claude to give you a post. You are asking Claude to give you 30 candidates so you can pick the 2 worth posting.

**Prompts:**

> Generate 30 hooks for this build: [paste: 2–3 sentences + proof numbers]. Audience: [one line]. My voice: [one line — what I sound like / key don'ts]. Across all 8 archetypes (Curiosity Gap, Contrarian, Stakes, Before/After, Little Promise, Revealed Mistake, Enemy, Status Shift) — ~4 per archetype. Force length variety: 10 short under 8 words, 10 medium under 16 words, 10 longer than 20 words. Be ruthlessly specific — every line should be a fact a stranger could verify, not a vague aspiration.

> Here are the hooks I liked and why: [paste]. Here are the ones I cut and why: [paste]. Generate 15 more hooks calibrated to the patterns in my winners — specifically not in the style of the ones I cut.

## ❌ The 6 Things That Kill A Hook — And The Fix For Each

![The 6 Things That Kill A Hook](/images/stage9-hook-killers.png)

> **You'll walk away with:** a diagnostic checklist of the 6 most common hook failure modes, with the rewrite move for each — so when a hook flops you can name why it flopped and fix it instead of guessing.

**Killer 1 — Vague Promise.** The hook makes a promise so general it could be about anything. "Here's how I built a great tool." Rewrite: add specifics — numbers, time, what it replaced. *"I built a $32 invoice-chaser that recovered $4,500 in 6 days."*

**Killer 2 — No Stake.** The hook describes a thing but doesn't tell the viewer why staying matters. "I built an automation tool." Rewrite: force the loss or gain into the first clause. *"I built an automation tool that's now saving me 3 hours/week."*

**Killer 3 — Greeting Or Throat Clearing.** "Hey guys, what's up, hope you're all doing well — so today I wanted to share..." Five seconds wasted on viewers who haven't decided to stay yet. Rewrite: delete the entire run-up. Start at the first interesting noun or number.

**Killer 4 — Title Disguised As Hook.** "5 Tips For Better Content" describes a list. It's not making a contract. Rewrite: turn the title into a story + stake. *"Same five tips — but I ran them in order on the same post, every day for 30 days. Here's which one actually compounded."*

**Killer 5 — AI-Voice / Adjective Soup.** "Revolutionary," "transformative," "absolutely game-changing." Readers clock AI language and disengage. Rewrite: replace every adjective with a concrete fact. *"A tool that replaced my $4,800/yr VA"* beats *"a groundbreaking automation solution."*

**Killer 6 — No Visual Anchor (video only).** The hook reads great on paper but on screen it's just moving lips with no text overlay until audio loads — and by then the thumb has moved. Rewrite: put the hook on screen as text in the first frame.

**When a hook flops, run the contract test:** *what specifically is this hook promising the viewer in the first three seconds, and does the body of the post pay that promise off?* If you can't answer both halves in plain words, the hook isn't fixed yet.

**Prompts:**

> Here are 3 hooks I wrote that underperformed: [paste]. For each, diagnose against these 6 killers: 1) Vague Promise, 2) No Stake, 3) Greeting/Throat-Clearing, 4) Title Disguised As Hook, 5) AI-Voice/Adjective Soup, 6) No Visual Anchor (video only). Name the killer(s) per hook, then rewrite the hook applying the specific fix. Plain-spoken builder voice.

> Here's a hook I'm about to post: [paste]. Pre-flight check it against all 6 killers. If it survives all 6, tell me. If not, name which killer is still present and rewrite once more. Be brutal — I'd rather kill the post than ship a weak hook.

## 🏦 Building Your Hook Bank — The Swipe File That Compounds

![Building Your Hook Bank](/images/stage9-hook-bank.png)

> **You'll walk away with:** a personal hook bank — your own swipe file of hooks that worked, hooks that didn't, and the data behind each — so future-you never starts from zero and every winning hook earns interest forever.

### The shape of the bank

A simple Google Sheet. Six columns, that's it:

- **Hook** — the actual first line, copy-paste ready
- **Archetype** — which of the 8 it is
- **Platform / Date** — where and when it was posted (or "never shipped" if it was cut)
- **Build** — one sentence on the underlying build
- **Result** — views, watch time, saves, likes, DMs. Numbers, not words.
- **Note** — one line of plain-language judgement: "Worked because..." or "killed because..."

That sixth column is the gold. It's what feeds back to Claude to improve next week's batch.

### How to use the bank

- **Open it at the start of every Hook Lab.** Before writing this week's 10 hooks, read your last 10 winning hooks. Notice what they have in common — those patterns are your fingerprint.
- **Once a month, read your kill pile.** The hooks you cut and never posted. Notice the patterns — those are the filters and traps you keep walking into.
- **Revisit old kills.** A hook that flopped 6 weeks ago might land now — your audience is bigger, your voice is sharper. Spotting the pattern in what you kill is how you upgrade your filter.

### What you have in 6 months

Six months of disciplined hook + build = roughly: 24 Hook Lab sessions, 168 hooks written, 72 sharpened, 48 tested, 12+ proven winners. A 240-row sheet that tells you exactly what your audience responds to, exactly what they don't, every archetype, every format pattern. The kill pile is not embarrassment — it's your edge.

**Prompts:**

## 🗣️ The Voice Manual — A Spec Every Draft Uses

> **You'll walk away with:** a personal voice manual — a 2,000-word spec that every drafting agent you run copies when it writes a word on your behalf — so your posts sound like you, not like the median LinkedIn voice.

A model trained on the whole internet defaults to the median. The median is bland. Your voice manual is the override — a high-specificity signal sourced from text you already shipped.

### The 9-point extraction template

Open your 3 highest-engagement posts — these are where your voice is loudest.

1. **Sentence rhythm** — short and punchy? Long and winding? Count words per sentence in your top 3 posts: that's your rhythm baseline.
2. **Pace** — do you front-load the payoff or build to it? Most builders lead with the result, not the journey.
3. **Analogies** — do you use them? Or do you replace analogies with screenshots and concrete proof?
4. **Emotional palette** — what your writing trades in. Confidence + warmth? Conviction + clarity? Irreverence + precision?
5. **Cultural references you'll actually use** — sports, music, film, specific founders. The reader should pattern-match in 5 seconds.
6. **Banned phrases** — the AI clichés you never want to ship. "Dive in." "Unlock." "Leverage." "Game-changer." Write 10+ verbatim.
7. **Signature opens** — your first-line patterns. "Here's the thing." "Real quick." A number. A confession.
8. **Signature closes** — how you end. "Onwards." One word. Period. Or just stop.
9. **Do-not-write list** — topics, jokes, takes that are off-limits. Politics, specific competitor names, anything off-brand.

### Where the file lives

Save it at `~/.claude/voice.md` so every Claude Code project on your machine reaches it. Before drafting any post, script, or message — Claude reads the real live file.

### The imitation test (verify it works)

Paste your voice manual into a fresh Claude context, then:

After the post, output: SELF-AUDIT — quote any sentence in your draft that violates a rule in @voice.md (or contains a banned phrase) and what you'd change. Don't grade yourself softly. If you can't write the post without breaking 2+ rules, the manual is the problem — tell me which 3 sections need more specifics.
