# STAGE 23 — SALES & DISTRIBUTION

## 📣 The Distribution Problem (And Why Most Builders Stay Broke)

You can build a perfect product with Claude Code in a weekend. It will sit there, unseen, making zero dollars, because building and selling are completely different skills — and most people who find Claude Code are builders, not sellers.

This stage is about the second skill. Not convincing people to buy something they don't want — that's hard and exhausting. Finding people who already have the problem your build solves, showing them you have the solution, and making it easy for them to say yes. That's it.

**The only three ways to get customers:**
1. **Outbound** — you reach out to them directly (cold DM, cold email, personal network)
2. **Inbound** — they find you (content, SEO, community, referrals)
3. **Paid** — you pay to reach them (ads, sponsorships)

Start with outbound. It's the only one that works in week one with zero audience and zero budget.

---

## 🎯 The One-Sentence Positioning Test

Before doing any outreach, you need to pass this test: can you explain what you built in one sentence that makes the right person say "I need that"?

**Template:**
> "I built [tool/service] that helps [specific type of person] [specific outcome] without [the thing they hate about current solutions]."

**Examples:**
> "I built an automation that helps local plumbers get 5-star Google reviews from every completed job — without asking awkwardly face-to-face."

> "I built a content system that turns a founder's weekly voice memo into 5 LinkedIn posts — without writing a single word."

If you can't fill in the template, your positioning isn't clear enough yet. Don't start outreach until you can pass this test.

## 📩 The Cold Outreach System

**Who to reach out to:** the specific person who has the problem your build solves. Not "small businesses" — plumbers in Austin with under 50 Google reviews. Not "content creators" — solo founders posting on LinkedIn 3+ times per week who still write their own posts.

**The cold DM formula (50 words max):**
```
Hey [Name] — noticed [specific observation about them].

I built something that [one-sentence value prop].

Worth a 15-min call to see if it fits? Happy to show you a live demo.
```

**What makes it work:**
- Specific observation = you actually looked at their profile (not a blast)
- One-sentence value prop = clear outcome, no fluff
- Low-commitment ask = 15 minutes, not "buy this"

**Volume:** send 20 DMs per day. Expect 1–3 responses. That's not a bad conversion — that's how cold outreach math works.

## 📧 Cold Email At Scale

For reaching larger audiences, cold email via **Apollo + Instantly** (covered in Stage 17) gives you the volume that DMs can't.

**The 3-email sequence:**

**Email 1 — The value-first intro:**
Subject: [Specific observation about their business]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Noticed [specific thing] — most [type of business] in [location] struggle with [problem].
>
> I built a tool that [outcome]. Happy to show you how it works on a 15-min call.
>
> Worth it?
>
> [Name]

**Email 2 — Follow-up (3 days later):**
> Just bumping this up in case it got buried.
>
> I also noticed [second observation] — that's usually a sign [problem] is costing real money.
>
> Still worth 15 mins?

**Email 3 — Final follow-up (5 days later):**
> Last one from me — I know the inbox gets crazy.
>
> If [problem] is something you're working on, I'd love to show you what we built.
>
> If not, no worries — I'll drop off your radar.

## 💬 Turning Conversations Into Clients

**The discovery call structure (30 minutes):**

1. **Warm-up (5 min):** ask about their business, their current workflow, what's working
2. **Pain discovery (10 min):** "How do you currently handle [the problem]?" "What does that cost you in time/money?" "What's the ideal state?"
3. **Demo (10 min):** show the build doing exactly what solves their pain. Don't show features — show outcomes.
4. **Close (5 min):** "Does this solve the problem you described?" → yes → "Here's how we'd work together: [price and process]."

**The rule:** don't pitch until you've confirmed the pain exists. The demo doesn't land if they don't feel the problem first.

## 🔁 Building Inbound Over Time

Outbound gets you your first 5–10 clients. Inbound scales you beyond what outbound can reach.

**The content → client flywheel:**

1. Build something useful
2. Document the build publicly (LinkedIn post, YouTube video, community thread)
3. People with the same problem find it
4. They reach out to you

**The fastest inbound channel for builders:** LinkedIn posts showing what you built, the problem it solved, and the result. No theory, no "here's what AI can do" — show the actual thing working.

**Format that performs:**
- Line 1: Bold claim or result (the hook)
- Lines 2–5: What you built and why
- Lines 6–10: How it works (briefly)
- Final line: Offer to share more / open to DMs

## 🤝 The Referral Machine

Your best clients send you their best contacts — if you ask.

**When to ask:** after a successful project delivery, when the client says something positive.

**The ask:**
> "I'm glad this worked well for you. Do you know anyone else in [industry] who has the same problem? I'd love an intro — I'll make sure they get the same result you did."

**Make it easy:** draft the intro email for them. They just need to forward it.

**Incentivize it:** offer a referral fee (10–20% of first project value) or a free month of service for any referral that converts.

## 📊 Track What Works

You cannot improve what you don't measure. A simple tracking system in Notion or Airtable:

| Date | Channel | Lead | Status | Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-07-01 | Cold DM | Plumber co. | Closed | $800 |
| 2026-07-02 | Referral | Agency | Demo booked | — |

Review weekly: which channel is converting? Which message gets responses? Double down on what works, cut what doesn't.
