# STAGE 18 — THE PROMPT VAULT

## ▶️ How To Use The Vault

The Prompt Vault is 160+ tested prompts organized into 16 categories. Each prompt is battle-tested — written, refined, and used on real builds and client projects.

**How to use it:**
1. Find the category that matches what you're trying to do right now
2. Copy the prompt as-is — they work out of the box
3. Fill in the brackets `[like this]` with your specifics
4. Paste into Claude Code or claude.ai

**The most important thing:** don't browse all 160 prompts. Pick the category you need today, use one prompt, and get a result. Come back when you need a different category.

## 🧑‍💻 Coding & Debugging — 10 Prompts

**1. Feature build:**
> "Build [feature] in [file]. Requirements: [list]. Constraints: don't change [what to preserve]. When done, confirm the build command passes."

**2. Bug fix:**
> "This error: [paste error]. File: [filename]. What caused it and what's the fix? One sentence explanation, then the corrected code."

**3. Code review:**
> "Review [file or paste code] as a senior engineer. List: bugs, edge cases, security issues, anything I'd be embarrassed to ship. Be direct."

**4. Refactor:**
> "Refactor [file] for readability. Don't change behavior. Break into functions if over 50 lines. Keep the same exports."

**5. Test writing:**
> "Write unit tests for [function/component]. Cover: happy path, empty input, error state. Use [testing framework]."

**6. TypeScript fix:**
> "Fix the TypeScript errors below. Keep the existing logic — only fix the types. Don't use `any`."

**7. API integration:**
> "Integrate [API name] into [file]. I need to: [what it should do]. Use the fetch API, not axios. Handle errors gracefully."

**8. Performance audit:**
> "Audit [file] for performance issues. What's slow, what causes unnecessary re-renders, and what's the highest-impact fix?"

**9. Documentation:**
> "Add JSDoc comments to [file]. Only document functions that aren't self-explanatory. Skip getters and simple utilities."

**10. Explain code:**
> "Explain what [function/file] does. Assume I know [language] but haven't touched this codebase. One paragraph, plain English."

## 🧠 Strategy & Decisions — 10 Prompts

**1. Decision framework:**
> "I need to decide: [decision]. Options: [list]. Criteria that matter most: [list]. Help me think through this systematically and give a recommendation."

**2. Pre-mortem:**
> "I'm about to [plan/decision]. Assume it fails. What are the 5 most likely reasons? How do I prevent each one?"

**3. Second-order effects:**
> "If I [decision/action], what are the second and third-order effects? What unintended consequences should I prepare for?"

**4. Opportunity cost:**
> "If I pursue [option A], what am I giving up? What does [option B] cost me by not doing it? Help me see the full tradeoff."

**5. Advisors panel:**
> "Imagine [3 specific advisors — e.g., Jeff Bezos, a cautious CFO, a growth hacker] are advising me on [decision]. What does each one say? Who would I listen to and why?"

**6. Stress test:**
> "Stress test this plan: [describe]. What assumptions am I making that could be wrong? What happens if each assumption fails?"

**7. Minimum viable test:**
> "What's the minimum viable test to validate [idea/hypothesis] in [timeframe] with [budget]? What would a positive result look like?"

**8. Exit criteria:**
> "I'm committing to [project/strategy] for [timeframe]. What are the criteria that would tell me it's not working and it's time to stop? Be specific — not 'it's not growing fast enough.'"

**9. Weekly strategic review:**
> "Here's what happened this week: [describe]. What's the strategic implication? What should I do differently next week? What's the one thing that matters most right now?"

**10. Long-term thinking:**
> "In 3 years, [current decision] will look [good/bad] if [what turns out to be true]. What needs to be true for this to be the right call? How do I find out?"
